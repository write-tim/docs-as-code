Every article in our Docs-as-Code knowledge base is built from two fundamental layers:

1. **Frontmatter Metadata:** Structured configuration attributes located at the top of the file.
2. **Body Content:** The narrative prose, headings, code snippets, and visual components that make up the article itself.

In traditional static site generators, authors must manually construct YAML syntax—where a single missing space or misplaced quote can crash the entire build. In Sveltia CMS, you interact with intuitive form fields that generate compliant frontmatter automatically.

---

## Frontmatter Fields Demystified

Here is a breakdown of the key metadata fields you will see at the top of the Sveltia editor:

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="1. Page Title (Required)" icon="document">
    **What it does:** Sets the primary `<h1>` heading on the page, the browser tab title, and the default link label in the sidebar navigation.  
    **Best Practice:** Keep titles clear, action-oriented, and under 60 characters.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2. Description (SEO & Search)" icon="magnifier">
    **What it does:** Powers search indexing in Pagefind, provides Google search engine meta tags, and generates social card previews when shared on Slack or LinkedIn.  
    **Best Practice:** Write 1–2 complete, engaging sentences summarizing the reader's takeaway.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3. Sidebar Sort Order" icon="list-format">
    **What it does:** Controls where this article appears in the left navigation tree. Lower numbers float to the top; higher numbers follow sequentially.  
    **Best Practice:** Use integers (`1, 2, 3...`) to structure a logical reading progression.
  </Card>
  <Card title="4. Sidebar Label & Hidden" icon="setting">
    **What it does:** Optionally overrides the title displayed in the sidebar (useful if the page title is long) or hides unlisted/draft guides from navigation altogether.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="tip" title="No Broken YAML Errors">
Because Sveltia renders inputs as native web controls (text fields, number steppers, and checkboxes), you never have to worry about indentation errors or escaping special characters like colons (`:`) or quotation marks (`"`). The CMS handles serialization safely.
</Aside>

## Why the Description Field Matters for SEO & Search

When users visit our documentation, they rely heavily on instant search (`Cmd+K`). 

Our search engine (**Pagefind**) indexes both the page content and the frontmatter `description`. When a reader searches for a term:
1. The **Title** provides immediate recognition.
2. The **Description** appears as the highlighted summary beneath the search result.

If an article omits a description, search engines and internal search tools must guess an excerpt from the first paragraph, which may be an introductory sentence rather than a concise summary of the page topic.

## Controlling Sidebar Navigation with Sort Order

The left sidebar in our documentation site is designed to present a logical, progressive learning journey:

<Steps>
1. **Overview / Fundamentals First:** Assign `order: 1` to introductory articles (e.g., *Welcome*, *Overview*, *Getting Started*).
2. **Sequential Tasks in the Middle:** Assign `order: 2`, `order: 3`, and `order: 4` to procedural or progressive authoring guides.
3. **Advanced Reference at the End:** Assign higher numbers to governance, advanced schemas, and syntax references.
</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Checking Existing Orders">
When viewing a collection list in Sveltia CMS, notice that article cards display their sort number in parentheses next to their title (e.g., `The Anatomy of an Article (2)`). This makes it easy to see which numbers are currently taken before assigning a new one.
</Aside>
